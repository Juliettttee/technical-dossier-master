# Adaptive Discovery Interview — prompt patterns

Use as examples, never a fixed questionnaire. Ask only 2–3 questions per turn and adapt to the user's answers.

## First round: uncertain user
1. What made you want to investigate this now: solving a real problem, deciding between options, or understanding how something works?
2. If you had only one valuable result from the report, what would it enable you to do?
3. Who will read it, and what background can we assume?

## Second round: probes based on an answer
- "You said you want technical details. Do you mainly need actual equipment/configuration, the scientific mechanism, or evidence that performance claims are reliable?"
- "If no original drawings are public, would verified descriptions plus clearly-labelled analytical schematics still help?"
- "You mentioned choosing a supplier. Is performance, compatibility, cost, compliance, or maintainability the decisive factor?"

## Challenge / reverse questioning
- "Which conclusion are you currently leaning towards, and what evidence would change your mind?"
- "What would make this dossier a failure, even if it looked professional?"
- "Do you need depth on one subsystem rather than shallow coverage of the whole project?"
- "Could the correct outcome be that publicly available information is insufficient?"
